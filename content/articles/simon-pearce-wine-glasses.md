## What to Look For in Wine Glasses

### Rim Thickness and How It Affects the Pour

The rim of a wine glass is the first thing that tells you whether it was made with drinking in mind or display in mind. A thin, rolled-under rim lets wine flow onto the palate cleanly, without the lip of the glass interrupting the experience. A thicker rim , common in tempered glass , creates a slight barrier you feel with every sip.

This distinction matters most with red wines, where the pour angle and glass shape work together to direct aromatics. Fine crystal, whether machine-made or handblown, typically achieves a rim thickness that tempered glass simply cannot match at scale.

### Bowl Shape and Varietal Matching

Not all wine glasses are interchangeable. A tall, broad bowl is designed to let Bordeaux varietals , Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot , breathe before the wine reaches your nose. A narrower, taller bowl suits white wines, concentrating delicate aromatics rather than dispersing them.

Universal glasses exist as a compromise: wide enough to work with most reds, narrow enough not to embarrass themselves with whites. For everyday entertaining, a single universal shape is practical. For serious wine service, a varietal-matched shape earns its keep.

### Weight and Balance in the Hand

A glass that feels too light reads as cheap even when it isn't. A glass that's too heavy tires the hand over a long dinner. The sweet spot is a glass with enough substance to signal quality when held, but balanced through the stem so the bowl feels controlled, not top-heavy.

Handblown glass introduces variation here , no two pieces are identical in weight. Machine-made crystal achieves precise consistency. Neither is objectively better; they represent different relationships to craft and uniformity.

### Durability and Practical Maintenance

Crystal, whether leaded or lead-free, is more fragile than tempered glass. Machine-made crystal tolerates the dishwasher better than handblown pieces, but even then, top-rack placement and no crowding are non-negotiable if you want the glasses to last.

Handblown glasses should always be hand-washed. If your hosting style involves loading the dishwasher after a party at midnight and not thinking about it, that's an important constraint to acknowledge before buying. ## How to Choose

### Decide Whether Craft Provenance Matters to Your Table

Some buyers want a glass that tells a story. Handblown American artisan glass , the Simon Pearce category , carries provenance that machine-made crystal simply cannot replicate. That's not a performance claim; it's a values claim. If the objects at your table reflect how you think about hospitality, the Barre set earns its place.

If your primary concern is consistency and reproducibility , matching sets, identical pours, predictable performance , machine-made crystal is the more rational answer. Both positions are coherent. Know which one you're buying for.

### Match the Glass to How You Actually Entertain

A buyer guide that ignores hosting scale is giving incomplete advice. If you regularly host six or more guests, the hand-wash requirement on artisan glass becomes a real logistical constraint. Budget-tier tempered glass that goes in the dishwasher may serve your table better than premium crystal that creates work at the end of the night.

Smaller, more intentional gatherings , four guests, a considered wine, a table set with attention , are where artisan glass performs at its best. The mismatch between glass quality and hosting context is one of the most common buying errors in this category. Match the purchase to the actual use.

### Understand Varietal-Specific Shapes Before You Buy

Buying a Bordeaux-specific glass without a working understanding of why the shape exists is a recipe for a cabinet full of glasses you don't reach for. The Riedel Vinum Bordeaux shape works because the bowl geometry directs wine toward the back of the palate , a specific effect for a specific style of wine.

If your table sees a mix of reds, whites, and sparkling over the course of a season, a universal-shape glass may serve you better than two or three varietal sets. The full range of [wine and entertaining glassware](/glassware/) categories maps this more granularly, and it's worth a look before committing to a specialized shape.

### Factor in Replacement Cost and Breakage Rate

Crystal breaks. Artisan crystal, when it breaks, is expensive to replace , and a handblown set where one glass is missing creates a visible gap at the table that a machine-made replacement can't fill. Budget-tier tempered glass breaks too, but the replacement cost is low and matching replacements are readily available.

Mid-range machine-made crystal sits in a reasonable middle position: durable enough that breakage is an occasional event rather than a regular one, replaceable at a cost that doesn't require a decision. Factor the full ownership period into the purchase, not just the initial outlay.

### Rim Feel as the Deciding Criterion

If you taste the difference a thin rim makes , and most people do once they've used fine crystal regularly , that sensation is hard to give up. The transition from crystal back to tempered glass at a restaurant or a friend's table is immediately apparent.

For buyers who have not yet made that comparison, the rim distinction may feel abstract. If you're uncertain, the Riedel Vinum set is the most efficient way to calibrate your own preference before investing in artisan glass or committing to budget-tier for volume entertaining.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are Simon Pearce wine glasses worth the premium over Riedel?
That depends entirely on what you're buying for. Riedel's Vinum line delivers superior varietal-specific performance , the bowl geometry is engineered for a purpose, and it works. Simon Pearce delivers craft provenance and artisan variation that machine-made crystal cannot replicate. If you want the best glass for a Bordeaux, Riedel wins.
### Can Simon Pearce glasses go in the dishwasher?
No. All handblown Simon Pearce glass is hand-wash only. The variation inherent in the handblown process , slight differences in wall thickness across each piece , makes thermal shock from a dishwasher a genuine risk. Hand-washing with warm water and a soft cloth is the correct care method.
### What is the difference between crystal and tempered glass in a wine glass?

Crystal , whether leaded or lead-free , is thinner at the rim and more resonant than tempered glass, and those properties affect both how a glass feels in use and how wine delivers to the palate. Tempered glass is thicker, heavier, and significantly more resistant to thermal shock and everyday impact. The [Bormioli Rocco Bistro set](product:bormioli-rocco-bistro-wine-glasses-set6) is the clearest illustration of what tempered glass does well. For everyday casual entertaining, the durability trade-off is often worth it.
### How many wine glasses do I need for a dinner party of six?

Eight is the practical answer , six for the table and two spare for breakage or an unexpected guest. If you're using a premium or artisan set, buying a set of four and supplementing with a more affordable option for the additional seats is a reasonable approach. Matching matters less to most guests than the quality of the glasses in front of them.
### Is the Riedel Vinum Bordeaux glass suitable for other red wines, or only Bordeaux?

The Bordeaux shape works well with any full-bodied, structured red , Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Malbec, and Syrah all benefit from the large bowl and the palate placement the shape creates. It's less suited to lighter reds like Pinot Noir, which perform better in a narrower, shorter bowl. The [Vinum Bordeaux](product:riedel-vinum-bordeaux-set2) is genuinely versatile across bold reds, but if your cellar skews toward Burgundy-style wines, a different shape serves you better.
