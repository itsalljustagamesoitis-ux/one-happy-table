Candle holders are one of those table details that either pull a setting together or disappear into the background entirely. Whether you're drawn to Michael Aram's sculptural metalwork or looking for something more neutral that still earns its place on the table, the choices in this category range from investment pieces to practical standbys. There's a full range of options worth exploring in [Decor & Candles](/decor/) before you commit to a style.

The difference between a candle holder that enhances a table and one that merely holds a candle comes down to finish, form, and how it reads in context. Scale matters, material matters, and so does whether the piece can hold its own without being surrounded by other things.

## What to Look For in a Candle Holder

### Form and Visual Weight

A candle holder does double duty , it holds a flame and it occupies visual space on your table. Those two roles don't always point toward the same choice. A sculptural piece with open metalwork reads like an art object even when unlit; a plain glass cylinder nearly disappears until there's a candle in it. Neither approach is wrong, but knowing which you're choosing changes how the piece functions in a setting.

Visual weight should be proportional to the table and the occasion. A heavy, detailed holder on a small breakfast table feels like an imposition. The same piece anchoring a long dinner table for eight feels exactly right.

### Finish and Material

Metal holders age differently than glass, and that aging matters more than most buyers expect. Oxidized metal develops a patina over time , with quality pieces, that's intentional and attractive. Polished finishes require more maintenance to stay looking intentional rather than just worn.

Mixed metal finishes , pieces combining silver and gold tones , are more versatile than single-finish pieces because they pick up both warm and cool tones in the room. If your table setting isn't fixed to one palette, a mixed-finish holder gives you more flexibility than a strictly warm or strictly cool piece.

### Stability

A candle holder that wobbles is not just an aesthetic problem. Any holder meant for tapers , the tall, narrow candles that tip easily , needs a base heavy enough to prevent accidental knock-over, especially at a table with movement, children, or any kind of activity. Check the base diameter relative to the candle height before buying.

Wider-base designs and holders with a weighted bottom compensate for a tall center of gravity. Decorative holders that prioritize sculptural form over base mass often rely on the taper itself sitting flush and deep in the socket , if the fit is loose, even a heavy holder can be a problem.

### Flame Protection

Open-air tapers look beautiful and burn unevenly. Any air movement , from a vent, an open window, guests moving around the table , shortens burn time and creates drips. A hurricane glass eliminates that problem entirely by enclosing the flame. The trade-off is the visual character of an open flame versus the protected look of a glass enclosure.

For outdoor dining or any table near an air source, a hurricane is almost always the more practical choice. For a still interior setting where the aesthetic of an open flame matters, a quality taper holder without a chimney is entirely appropriate.

### Cleaning and Maintenance

This is the criterion most buyers skip and later regret. Intricate metalwork with layered leaf and petal details collects wax, dust, and residue in every crevice. A plain glass cylinder is one rinse away from clean. The more sculptural the piece, the more deliberate you need to be about upkeep , especially if you use it regularly rather than for occasions only.

Wax residue on metal is best removed with warm (not hot) water and a soft brush, not by picking at the detail work. If the idea of that maintenance feels like a chore rather than a small ritual, a simpler holder serves you better. Browsing the full range in [candle holders and table decor](/decor/) first can help calibrate where you actually land on that trade-off.

## How to Choose

### Decide Whether You're Buying an Object or a Function

The single most useful question before buying a candle holder: is the holder itself meant to be seen, or is it just meant to hold a candle? A Michael Aram piece is table art that happens to hold a candle , people will notice it, comment on it, and it carries meaning separate from the flame. A glass hurricane is a functional vessel , no one will remark on the holder itself, only on the atmosphere the candlelight creates.

Neither is the wrong answer. But conflating the two leads to buyers spending premium money on a sculptural piece they then obscure with flowers, or buying a plain holder and feeling disappointed that it doesn't add to the table.

### Match the Piece to the Occasion Frequency

A holder you use every week lives a different life than one you bring out for holidays and dinner parties. For frequent use, cleanability matters enormously , open metalwork that requires careful cleaning around every detail adds up over time. Glass cleans in minutes. For occasional use, maintenance becomes a minor consideration and the sculptural and aesthetic qualities of a premium piece justify themselves more easily.

If you're outfitting a table for regular family dinners, a practical holder that can be cleaned quickly and stored without ceremony is almost always the right choice. If you're buying something for a table that gets set intentionally and put away with care, the investment in a designed object pays off.

### Consider the Candle Type

Taper candles and pillar candles have completely different holder requirements. Tapers need a socket that fits the candle diameter precisely , too loose and the taper leans; too tight and seating it damages the candle. Pillar candles need a flat or slightly recessed surface, not a socket. Most decorative holders are designed for one or the other, not both.

The hurricane functions as a taper holder with a glass chimney. Verify the candle type before purchasing, and check whether the holder specifies a standard taper diameter.

### Think About What's Already on Your Table

A highly sculptural candle holder on a table already busy with patterned china, layered linens, and a dense floral arrangement reads as visual noise. The same holder on a spare, minimal table setting is exactly right. Before buying a statement piece, assess the visual density of your existing table , is there room for something that commands attention, or does the table already have enough going on?

This kind of table-planning thinking is worth doing with your broader [decorating and tabletop choices](/decor/) at the same time, since candle holders rarely sit in isolation and their impact depends entirely on context.

### Budget Against Longevity

A well-made metal candle holder outlasts most things on a table. The design vocabulary of Michael Aram pieces specifically is not trend-dependent , the organic, nature-motif aesthetic has been consistent for decades and reads as classic rather than fashionable. That longevity changes the value calculation: a premium piece bought once, used for years, and stored carefully costs less over time than a series of budget replacements.

The glass hurricane occupies a different position , inexpensive enough that replacing it when it eventually breaks or clouds isn't a financial consideration. Both can be the right answer depending on how you want to think about the purchase.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are Michael Aram candle holders worth the premium price?

For buyers who want a table object with genuine design identity, yes. Michael Aram pieces are hand-crafted and use a sculptural vocabulary that reads as art rather than accessory. The value case depends on whether the aesthetic matters to you , if you want the brand's distinctive organic metalwork on your table, there's no close substitute at a lower price point. If you want candlelight without a statement object, a glass hurricane at a budget price point delivers the function without the investment.
### Can the Butterfly Ginkgo candlestick holders be used outdoors?

The open metalwork design makes these better suited to indoor settings. Any wind will affect an unprotected taper, and outdoor conditions accelerate the oxidation of the metal finish in ways that aren't always attractive. If you're entertaining outdoors and want candlelight, a hurricane holder is a more practical choice , the glass chimney protects the flame and the enclosed cylinder is more resistant to conditions than open metalwork.
### What candle size fits a standard taper holder?

Most taper holders, including the Michael Aram pieces, are designed for standard taper diameters , generally three-quarters of an inch at the base. Tapers vary slightly by brand, so a loose fit can be corrected with a small taper adapter or a small piece of foil around the base. Always seat the taper fully into the socket before lighting, and never leave burning candles unattended regardless of how stable the holder is.
### How do I clean wax residue from intricate metalwork?

Let the wax cool and harden completely before attempting to clean , picking at warm wax deforms the metalwork. Once hardened, most wax can be removed gently with a wooden skewer or soft brush. For residue in deep crevices, a small amount of warm water with mild soap and a soft-bristle brush works well. Avoid harsh chemicals or abrasive pads, which can damage the oxidized finish that gives Michael Aram pieces their distinctive character.
### Is the large hurricane holder stable enough for a dinner table with young children?

A glass cylinder holder carries real breakage risk in any active setting, and tall glass near children and table movement deserves honest assessment. The hurricane's weight provides reasonable stability on a flat surface, but a knock from an active table can tip it. For family tables, lower and wider holders are generally safer than tall glass cylinders. If candlelight matters to you in that setting, battery-operated flameless candles inside the hurricane give you the aesthetic without the safety concern.
