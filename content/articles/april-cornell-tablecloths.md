April Cornell tablecloths have a following for a reason , the botanical prints, the soft cotton voile, the way a table set with one of their cloths looks genuinely considered rather than just covered. But the brand is specific, and not every table or occasion calls for that much pattern. Choosing well means knowing what you actually need from a tablecloth before you fall for the print. A good place to start is the full range of [table linens](/linens/) available at different price points and in different styles.

The three picks below cover the main decisions most buyers face: a signature April Cornell floral for the reader who wants pattern and character, a seasonal runner for practical holiday entertaining, and a premium hemstitched cloth for the buyer who wants something quieter and built to last.

## What to Look For in a Tablecloth

### Fabric and Drape

Cotton tablecloths drape differently from polyester blends, and that difference is visible from across the room. Cotton voile , the construction April Cornell uses , falls softly and creates gentle folds at the table edge. It reads as intentional rather than stiff. The trade-off is care: cotton wrinkles. If you're not willing to iron or steam before a dinner party, a wrinkle-resistant blend will serve you better on a weeknight than a fine cotton will.

Linen and linen-cotton blends occupy a middle ground. They soften over time, wrinkle less aggressively than pure cotton voile, and hold up well to repeated washing. Egyptian cotton , used by manufacturers like Peacock Alley , brings long staple fibers that resist pilling and maintain surface smoothness through years of laundering.

### Pattern Scale and Table Composition

A large-scale floral print commands the table. That's a deliberate choice, and it works beautifully with simple white or cream dinnerware, unadorned stemware, and a low centerpiece that doesn't compete. Where it breaks down is when the tablecloth, the dinnerware, and the centerpiece are all doing something visually complicated at the same time.

Before buying a patterned cloth, set your usual dinnerware on the table and ask whether there's room for one more strong visual element. If the answer is no, a solid-colored or subtly textured cloth will likely serve the table better. Pattern works best when it's the one thing you've decided to feature.

### Size and Drop

Drop , the amount of fabric that hangs below the table edge , changes the formality of a setting. A generous drop of twelve to fourteen inches reads as formal. A shorter drop of six to eight inches is casual and practical for households with children. Neither is wrong; they signal different things.

Measure your table before buying, and account for drop when reading size charts. A tablecloth listed as fitting a 60x84 table may assume a six-inch drop on all sides. If you want more coverage, size up. Exploring the full range of [table linens](/linens/) by size is the most reliable way to avoid returns.

### Construction and Finishing Details

Hemstitching , rows of drawn threads finished with a decorative stitch , is a traditional detail on fine table linens. It adds visual interest without introducing color or pattern, which makes it compatible with almost any table composition. On cheaper cloths, hemstitching is often machine-mimicked rather than hand-finished, and the difference is visible: the spacing is uneven and the threads pucker.

Look at the hem corners. A well-made tablecloth has mitered corners , the fabric folds neatly at forty-five degrees rather than bunching. It's a small detail that indicates the overall quality of construction.

## How to Choose

### Pattern or No Pattern

This is the decision that drives everything else. A tablecloth with a strong print , particularly a large-scale botanical like the April Cornell , works when the rest of the table is simple enough to support it. Plain white or cream dinnerware, minimal centerpiece, unadorned stemware. If your existing dinnerware is patterned or your centerpiece is elaborate, a solid or subtly detailed cloth will hold the table together better than a competing print.

The runner is its own category: it layers pattern over an existing cloth or bare surface, and it allows you to shift the mood seasonally without replacing your full table linen setup.

### Occasion and Frequency

A premium cloth like the Peacock Alley invites careful handling , laundering it less frequently and treating it as the good linen you bring out for formal occasions. That's appropriate for some households and impractical for others.

If you host frequently and want to put the tablecloth in the wash after every dinner without worrying about it, a wrinkle-resistant runner or a cotton blend rated for regular machine washing is more suitable. Match the cloth to the way you actually host, not to an idealized version of it.

For holiday-specific hosting , Thanksgiving through New Year , the tartan runner earns its place precisely because it's seasonal. You use it for six to eight weeks, store it, and pull it out again next year. The investment is appropriate to the use.

### Sizing for Your Table

Standard rectangular dining tables run 36 inches wide and 60, 72, or 84 inches long. Most tablecloths list the table size they fit, which assumes a drop of six to eight inches on each end. If you want a longer drop for a more formal look, size up by one step.

Round and oval tables require separate sizing charts. Don't estimate , measure the table and compare against the manufacturer's stated drop. Returning a tablecloth because the drop was two inches shorter than expected is avoidable.

### Fiber and Care Reality

Cotton voile and Egyptian cotton are genuinely beautiful fabrics. They're also more demanding in care than polyester blends. If ironing before a dinner party sounds like a reasonable step in your routine, either of those will reward the effort. If it doesn't, buy accordingly and don't buy a fabric that will frustrate you every time you use it.

Wrinkle-resistant blends have improved significantly. They won't feel or drape exactly like fine cotton, but they photograph reasonably well, launder easily, and don't require preparation time. For weeknight entertaining, that's often the better answer. The full range of [table linen](/linens/) options , from casual to formal , is worth reviewing before settling on one cloth for every occasion.

### Color and Season

Red and green work hard for six weeks and then need to be retired. Dusty blue, ivory, white, and warm neutrals work year-round. If you're building a small collection of table linens rather than buying for a single occasion, start with a neutral cloth that covers most of the year and add a seasonal runner for the periods when you want more atmosphere.

That approach , a quality year-round cloth plus one or two seasonal runners , is more versatile than multiple full tablecloths in occasion-specific colors, and usually more economical in the long run.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is April Cornell a good brand for tablecloths?

April Cornell makes genuinely well-designed tablecloths with distinctive botanical prints and soft cotton voile construction. The brand is worth buying if you want pattern and character at the table and are willing to iron the cloth before use. For buyers who want plain or neutral linens, April Cornell's specific aesthetic may not be the right fit, but within its category it's consistently well-executed.
### How do I keep a cotton tablecloth from wrinkling?

Remove the tablecloth from the dryer while it's still slightly damp and lay it flat or hang it immediately , this reduces set-in creases significantly. Iron or steam on a medium-heat setting before placing it on the table. For the best results with a cotton voile cloth like the April Cornell Christmas Rose, iron in the direction of the grain rather than in circles, which can stretch the fabric.
### Can a table runner replace a full tablecloth?

A runner works well over a bare wood or stone table for casual and seasonal settings, or layered over a plain tablecloth for a more composed look. It won't provide the full coverage or formality of a tablecloth, but for everyday holiday hosting the [Tartan Plaid Table Runner 14 x 72](product:table-runner-tartan-plaid-14x72) offers a practical middle ground , pattern and seasonal atmosphere without the laundering overhead of a full cloth.
### What's the difference between the April Cornell tablecloth and the Peacock Alley hemstitch?

They serve different table aesthetics. The April Cornell Christmas Rose is all about pattern , a bold botanical print in holiday red that becomes the focal point of the table. The [Peacock Alley Hemstitch Tablecloth](product:peacock-alley-hemstitch-tablecloth) is a quiet, formally finished cloth that lets the dinnerware and centerpiece lead. Choose April Cornell if you want the cloth to make the statement; choose Peacock Alley if you want the cloth to support the rest of the table without competing.
### How much drop should a tablecloth have?

For a formal dinner setting, aim for twelve to fourteen inches of drop on each side , enough fabric to drape naturally without pooling on the floor. For casual family meals, six to eight inches is practical and still looks intentional. Measure your table before buying rather than relying on a general size description, since manufacturers vary in what "fits a 60x84 table" actually means in terms of finished dimensions.
