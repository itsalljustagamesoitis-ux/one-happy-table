Royal blue has a way of making a table feel considered , the kind of color choice that reads as intentional rather than incidental. Whether you're dressing a long banquet table for a wedding reception or setting something formal for a holiday dinner, the tablecloth carries more visual weight than most people expect until they get it wrong. The full range of [table linens](/linens/) covers every style and fabric type, but this guide focuses on three tablecloths I'd actually put on my own table.

The difference between a tablecloth that looks polished and one that looks afterthought isn't always fabric quality , it's fit, drape, and how the color holds up to candlelight and china. Those are the criteria that shaped every pick below.

## What to Look For in a Tablecloth

### Size and Drop

Getting the size wrong is the most common tablecloth mistake, and it's an easy one to avoid with a little math. The drop , the distance from the table edge to the hem , determines whether a tablecloth looks formal or casual. A 6- to 8-inch drop reads as everyday; a 10- to 12-inch drop reads as dressed. For a full formal drop that reaches within a few inches of the floor, you need a drop closer to 15 inches on each side.

Measure your table before you buy anything. A standard 8-foot banquet table runs 96 inches long and typically 30 inches wide. A 90x132-inch tablecloth gives you enough length to cover it completely and still deliver a respectable drop. For a round table, the formula is simpler: diameter plus twice the drop you want equals the cloth diameter you need.

If you're renting banquet tables for an event, confirm the dimensions in advance , rental tables vary more than most people realize.

### Fabric and Drape

The fabric determines everything downstream: how the cloth falls, how it holds a pressed crease, how it survives a dinner service. Egyptian cotton is the gold standard for formal tablecloths because the longer fibers produce a smooth, dense weave that drapes with genuine weight. Polyester-blend tablecloths are more forgiving on maintenance but tend to look flat under close inspection.

Linen is the other serious option. It drapes differently than cotton , a little softer in the fold, slightly more casual in character even when perfectly ironed. For very formal tables, cotton holds the starched-and-pressed aesthetic better. For a relaxed but still elevated look, linen is difficult to beat.

Don't overlook weight. A tablecloth that's too light will shift during service. A cloth with enough body stays put and holds its layout through an entire evening.

### Color and Pattern Considerations

Navy and royal blue read differently on a table. Royal blue is brighter and more saturated , it reads as celebratory and pairs naturally with gold or white china. Navy skews formal and works well with silver flatware and crisp white napkins. Neither is wrong, but they create different moods and it's worth being deliberate about which you want.

Solid colors show every crease and every piece of lint. If you're choosing a deep blue for a formal setting, plan to press the cloth before the table is set and have a lint roller nearby. Neutral tones , ivory, white, natural linen , give you more flexibility because they function as background rather than statement, letting the china and centerpiece carry the color story.

Exploring the full selection of [formal table linens](/linens/) before settling on a color can help you see how different tones interact with the other elements you're already working with.

### Construction Details

Hemstitching, mitered corners, and finished selvage edges are the construction details that separate a tablecloth that looks handsome from one that looks cheap. A hemstitched border , rows of drawn-thread work along the hem , adds a formal decorative element without relying on pattern. It's a traditional detail that reads well in both traditional and contemporary table settings.

Look at the corners. A properly mitered corner lies flat and holds its shape. A poorly finished corner bunches or lifts, and no amount of pressing fixes it. Pull the cloth tight at the corner during inspection , if it doesn't lie flat, it won't lay flat on your table either.

## How to Choose

### Match the Drop to the Occasion

The drop length is the single most powerful signal your tablecloth sends about formality level. A 6-inch drop is appropriate for a casual dinner. A 12-inch drop is dressed. A full-floor drop is event-formal , reserved for wedding receptions and formal banquets where the table base is meant to disappear entirely.

Decide the formality level first, then back into the cloth size. Don't start with the tablecloth and hope the drop works out. Once you know the drop you want and have your table dimensions, the math takes about thirty seconds.

### Consider Your China Before Your Tablecloth Color

The tablecloth's job is to support the china, not compete with it. If you own a statement china pattern , something with a strong color or motif , a neutral tablecloth lets the china lead. If your china is white or very simple, a colored tablecloth gives the table its personality and the china can hold its own against the competition.

Royal and navy blue work particularly well with white or gold-rimmed china. They also work with natural wood chargers and woven textures if you're building a less formal but still elevated look. Ivory and neutral tablecloths from the [table linens](/linens/) category give you the most flexibility across different china patterns and seasonal centerpieces.

### Fabric for the Way You Actually Entertain

If you're hosting once or twice a year for occasions where the table needs to look polished, Egyptian cotton is worth the care it requires. The drape and finish justify the ironing. If you're setting tables regularly , hosting monthly or using tablecloths for recurring events , consider whether your laundering setup can realistically support a cotton care routine.

For high-frequency event use, a well-made polyester-blend tablecloth in the right size is a legitimate choice. For the formal table you're setting four times a year for people who will notice the difference, cotton and fine linen are the right investment.

### Buying for a Set Versus a Single Cloth

If you're building a set , multiple matching tablecloths for a long table arrangement, or cloths that will be used together regularly , buy everything from the same production run. Color consistency in textiles is never guaranteed across different production batches, and a set of tablecloths that don't quite match is more visible than a deliberate mix.

This matters most for premium textiles like the Peacock Alley hemstitch. Buy the quantity you need in a single order, and store any extras carefully.

### Care Instructions Are Part of the Product Evaluation

Read the care label before you buy, not after the first wash goes wrong. Egyptian cotton can be laundered at home, but it requires low heat and careful pressing. Some premium tablecloths recommend dry cleaning , factor that into your ongoing cost calculation if you're hosting frequently.

Stain treatment is worth knowing in advance, too. A tablecloth that requires immediate cold-water treatment for red wine stains is a different product than one that tolerates a normal wash cycle. If you're hosting events with unpredictable guests, factor washability into your decision alongside drape and finish.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What's the difference between royal blue and navy blue for a tablecloth?

Royal blue is a brighter, more saturated color , it reads as festive and works well for celebratory events like weddings and holiday parties. Navy sits darker on the spectrum and reads as more formal and restrained, pairing naturally with silver flatware and white dinnerware. Neither is superior; they create different moods, and the choice should be driven by the occasion and the china you're using.
### What size tablecloth fits an 8-foot banquet table?

A 90x132-inch tablecloth is the standard fit for an 8-foot banquet table, delivering a drop of approximately 15 inches on each long side , enough for a formal presentation without reaching the floor. The [LinenTablecloth Navy Blue Tablecloth 90x132](product:linen-tablecloth-navy-blue-90x132) is sized precisely for this application. If you want a full floor-length drop for an event, you'll need a wider cloth or a table skirt to supplement.
### Is Egyptian cotton worth the extra effort over a polyester tablecloth?

For a formal dinner table that you're setting a few times a year, yes. Egyptian cotton drapes with a weight and finish that polyester blends genuinely cannot replicate, and it holds a pressed crease through an entire evening. The trade-off is real: it requires careful ironing and attention to laundering. For high-frequency event use or situations where you're washing tablecloths after every event, a well-made polyester blend is a practical alternative.
### How do I keep a dark tablecloth lint-free before guests arrive?

A lint roller is the most effective tool , go over the cloth after it's on the table, working in long strokes in the direction of the weave. Dark tablecloths like navy blue show lint, pet hair, and even dust much more than lighter colors, so make the lint roller part of your setup routine rather than an afterthought. Setting the table as close to the arrival time as possible also helps minimize what settles on the cloth.
### Can I use the Peacock Alley Hemstitch Tablecloth for an outdoor event?

Peacock Alley's hemstitch construction is designed for indoor formal dining , the Egyptian-cotton fabric and drawn-thread hemstitch detail are not suited to outdoor conditions involving wind, moisture, or direct sun exposure. For outdoor events, look for purpose-built outdoor tablecloths with weighted corners or clips to prevent movement. The hemstitch tablecloth belongs on a properly set indoor table where the construction detail is visible and the fabric is protected from the elements.
